SMS Short Codes: Why Some Verification Codes Never Reach Virtual Numbers

Short answer

A short code is a 5- or 6-digit number that companies use to send text messages in bulk, including verification codes. Short-code messages travel over carrier routes that many virtual number apps do not receive, and they only work inside one country, so codes sent this way often never arrive on a VoIP or foreign number.

How it works

The basics

When a bank, retailer or app texts you a code from a number like 32665 or 227898, that sender is a short code. In the US, short codes are leased through a central registry and approved by each mobile carrier before use. Companies like them because they can send large volumes of messages quickly and reliably to normal mobile phones.

How it works

Why they fail

Short codes were built for mobile carrier networks. Delivery depends on agreements between the sender's provider and each carrier, and many internet number providers are not part of those routes. The result: the message is accepted by the sender, then silently dropped before it reaches a VoIP number.

Short codes are also national. A US short code cannot reach a UK or Australian number, so a service that only sends codes by US short code cannot verify a foreign number at all.

How it works

How to recognise it

  • Normal texts from friends arrive on the number, but codes from one particular company never do.
  • The same service sends codes fine to your mobile number.
  • Past messages from that company in your mobile inbox came from a 5- or 6-digit sender.

How it works

What to try

  • Ask for a voice call if the service offers one.
  • Use email or an authenticator app where the service allows it instead of SMS.
  • Use a number in the same country as the service sending the code.
  • Pick a different number for that service from our ranking if it keeps failing.

FAQ

Questions People Ask

Can Google Voice receive short codes?

Often not. Many short-code senders do not deliver to Google Voice, which is a common reason codes from banks never arrive there.

Why do I get normal texts but not verification codes?

Normal texts come from regular numbers. Verification codes often come from short codes, which use different routes that many virtual numbers do not receive.

Can a short code reach a number in another country?

No. Short codes work inside one country only.
