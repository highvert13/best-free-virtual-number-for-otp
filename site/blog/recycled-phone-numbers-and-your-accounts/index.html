Recycled Phone Numbers: What Happens to Your Accounts When You Lose a Number

Short answer

When you stop using a phone number, the provider eventually gives it to someone else. Any account that still uses that number for login codes or recovery can then be reached by the new owner. Before you let a number go, move every account to another number or, better, to an authenticator app.

Privacy & safety

How recycling works

Phone numbers are a limited resource, so carriers and number apps reuse them. Free number apps usually reclaim a number after a period without calls or texts. Mobile carriers reassign numbers after a contract ends. The next person to get the number receives every text sent to it, including verification codes meant for you.

Researchers at Princeton University looked at recycled mobile numbers in 2021 and found that a large share of them were still linked to the previous owner's online accounts.

Privacy & safety

How to protect yourself

  1. Keep free numbers active

    Most free apps reclaim numbers after inactivity. Send a text or make a call regularly while you still use the number.

  2. List what uses the number

    Check messaging apps, email, banking, shopping and social accounts for this phone number.

  3. Switch to an authenticator app

    Where a service offers it, replace SMS codes with an authenticator app or a passkey.

  4. Update recovery options

    Change the recovery phone to a number you will keep, and add a recovery email.

  5. Only then let the number go

    Remove the number from each account before you close the app or cancel the line.

FAQ

Questions People Ask

How long until a free number is reassigned?

It depends on the app. Many reclaim numbers after a few weeks or months without activity, so check the app's own rules.

Can someone get into my accounts with my old number?

Yes, if the account still uses that number for login codes or password resets.

Is a public receive-SMS number already recycled?

Effectively yes: it is shared with everyone who visits the site, all the time.
