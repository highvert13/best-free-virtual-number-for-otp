Authenticator Apps vs SMS Codes: Which Two-Factor Method Is Safer?

Short answer

SMS codes are better than no second factor, but they can be intercepted through SIM swaps, recycled numbers and lost virtual numbers. Authenticator apps and passkeys are tied to your device instead of a phone number, which makes them safer. Use SMS codes for signup, and switch important accounts to an app or passkey.

Privacy & safety

Why SMS is the weakest

An SMS code proves you control a phone number, not that you are you. Numbers can be moved to a criminal's SIM in a SIM swap, reassigned to a new owner when you stop paying, or reclaimed by a free app. In each case the codes go to someone else. The US National Institute of Standards and Technology has treated SMS as a restricted authentication method for this reason.

Virtual numbers add another risk: if the app reclaims the number, or the number is public, you lose the second factor completely.

Privacy & safety

When each one fits

  • SMS codes: creating an account, low-value services, and services that offer nothing else.
  • Authenticator app: email, social media, work accounts and anything you can't afford to lose.
  • Passkey: any service that supports it; it also replaces your password.

A practical approach with a virtual number: use it to sign up, then turn on an authenticator app and remove the phone number from the account's security settings.

FAQ

Questions People Ask

Is SMS two-factor authentication still worth using?

Yes, it is much better than a password alone. It is just weaker than an authenticator app or a passkey.

What happens if I lose the phone with my authenticator app?

Use the backup codes you saved when you set it up, or an app that syncs encrypted backups. Save backup codes for every important account.

Should I use a virtual number for two-factor authentication?

Only if it is a private number you will keep. For important accounts an authenticator app is safer.
